CEFR A2 · Adult Learners

Making Small Talk at Work

The Problem

Adult English learners at the A2 level frequently know grammar and vocabulary but freeze when it comes to the unscripted, culturally-loaded moments before a meeting starts. In a 60-minute private lesson setting, learners need targeted practice distinguishing which topics are safe for workplace small talk versus which carry cultural risk (e.g., politics, money, religion), along with the specific transition language used to shift from casual chat into a formal agenda.

Highlights

CEFR Calibration Activity Design Teacher-Facing Copy Instructional Scaffolding Culturally-Responsive Content Design

Learning Objectives

  • Identify which small talk topics are appropriate versus inappropriate for the workplace.
  • Recall and use key vocabulary and questions from a modeled small talk dialogue between two co-workers.
  • Create an original small talk dialogue that includes three suitable topics and a natural transition into a meeting.

Deliverables

  • Student Version (PDF) — warm-up questions, listening/reading script, four language-focus practice tasks, and a free-production role-play activity.
  • Teacher's Guide (PDF) — full procedure, scaffolding notes, answer key, and pedagogical rationale.
  • Credits & Attributions (PDF) — image and audio/script sourcing information.
  • Embedded audio: a one-minute native-paced dialogue for the listening task.

Tools Used

Microsoft Word / Google Docs Canva Perplexity AI (editorial review & drafting support)

Grade 12 · High School Literature

The Gift of the Magi

The Problem

High school seniors often read literary classics passively, extracting plot without engaging in the language work, self-reflection, or spoken exchange that turns a story into a lasting lesson. In a 120-minute paired lesson, learners need structured support to decode O. Henry's dense, ironic vocabulary in context, process the story's twist ending critically, and connect it to their own lived experience through speaking and writing, not just comprehension questions.

Highlights

Integrated Skills Design Paired Reading Structure Vocabulary-in-Context Literary Reflection Compare/Contrast Language Focus

Learning Objectives

  • Use context clues to figure out the meaning of tricky words and phrases from the story.
  • Explain how the surprise ending of "The Gift of the Magi" made them feel.
  • Evaluate whether the characters in the story handled the situation well at the end.
  • Compose a brief reflection about a meaningful gift-exchange experience from their own life.
  • Compare and contrast their gift-giving experience with a partner's and with the story, using words like "similarly," "in contrast," and "unlike."

Deliverables

  • Student Version (PDF) — vocabulary warm-up, compare/contrast language study, paired reading activity, and a three-part reflection and sharing sequence.
  • Teacher's Guide (PDF) — full procedure, timing breakdown, scaffolding notes, answer key, and pedagogical rationale.
  • Paired Reading Chunks (PDF) — the story divided into alternating turns for two readers, keeping pacing and participation balanced.
  • Credits & Attributions (PDF) — public domain sourcing information for the story text.

Tools Used

Microsoft Word / Google Docs Canva Perplexity AI (editorial review & drafting support)

Grades 1–2 · Phonics

What Luke Can Do–What Luke Did Yesterday

The Problem

First- and second-grade learners often read "-ed" words as if they had one sound, but the ending is spoken three ways: /t/, /d/ and /əd/. This teacher-led, 45-minute phonics lesson connects each base verb to its past-tense form through a short decodable story about Luke the friendly dino (inspired by my 4 year old nephew). Students read, sort, hear and spell the endings in context.

Highlights

Decodable reader 45-minute lesson Grades 1–2 UFLI-inspired routines Differentiation and extension Built-in assessment

Learning Objectives

  • Read a base word and its "-ed" form.
  • Listen to the last sound in the base word.
  • Say whether "-ed" sounds like /t/, /d/ or /əd/ by listening to the last sound in the base word.
  • Spell a sentence that includes an "-ed" word.

Deliverables

  • At-A-Glance (1 pg, PDF)
  • Teacher's guide (8 pgs, PDF)
  • Student decodable: "What Luke Can Do–What Luke Did Yesterday" (10 pgs, PDF)
  • Teacher decodable: "What Luke Can Do–What Luke Did Yesterday" (10 slides, PPT)
  • Credits and attributions (1 pg, PDF)

Tools Used

Perplexity AI (image generation and editorial feedback) UFLI Foundations (inspiration and guidance) Microsoft Word and PDF export PowerPoint Canva

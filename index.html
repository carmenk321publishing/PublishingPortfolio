CEFR A2 · Adult Learners

Making Small Talk at Work

Adult English learners at the A2 level frequently know grammar and vocabulary but freeze when it comes to the unscripted, culturally-loaded moments before a meeting starts. In a 60-minute private lesson setting, learners need targeted practice distinguishing which topics are safe for workplace small talk versus which carry cultural risk (e.g., politics, money, religion), along with the specific transition language used to shift from casual chat into a formal agenda.

Design Rationale

The lesson follows an Engage → Study → Activate structure: a low-stakes warm-up builds comfort with the topic, a modeled script and guided practice tasks build recognition and recall, and a final free-production task lets learners apply the language in an original, personalized context.

Grade 12 · High School Literature

The Gift of the Magi

High school seniors often read literary classics passively, extracting plot without engaging in the language work, self-reflection, or spoken exchange that turns a story into a lasting lesson. In a 120-minute paired lesson, learners need structured support to decode O. Henry's dense, ironic vocabulary in context, process the story's twist ending critically, and connect it to their own lived experience through speaking and writing, not just comprehension questions.

Design Rationale

The lesson follows an Engage → Study → Activate structure: a vocabulary warm-up builds confidence with the story's dense 1905 language before reading begins, a dedicated compare/contrast language study equips learners with the connectors they'll need later, and the paired reading itself turns a traditionally solitary task into a shared, accountable exchange. The reflection and sharing sequence then activates all four language skills in one arc: learners write about the story and their own experience, speak it aloud to a partner, and finally synthesize both using the target comparison language. This integration is why the lesson is framed as skills-based rather than reading-only: the story is the anchor, but reading, writing, speaking, and listening are all deliberately built into equal, connected stages.
